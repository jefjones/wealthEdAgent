Court Ordered Dependency Coverage
When a parent who is eligible for family health coverage is required by a court order to provide health insurance coverage for a child,
an accident and health insurer may not deny enrollment on the grounds that the child:

* Was born out of wedlock and is entitled to coverage an insurer of a noncustodial parent;

* Was born out of wedlock and the custodial parent seeks enrollment for the child under that parent's policy;

* Is not claimed as a dependent on the parent's federal tax return; or

* Does not reside with the parent or in the insurer's service area.
Eligibility of Dependent Children Not Based Solely on Residency
A group policy that provides coverage to children of group members cannot deny eligibility for coverage to a child solely because the
child does not reside with the group member or solely because the child is completely dependent on a former spouse of the group
member rather than on the group member. A child who does not reside with the group member may be excluded on the same basis as
children who do reside with the group member.
Policy Extension for Handicapped Children
The term “disabled dependent" refers to a child who is

* Unable to engage in gainful employment - due to a physical or mental impairment that may result in death or last for a period of

at least 12 months; and

* Chiefly dependent upon an insured for support.
If a policy covers a disabled dependent, it cannot terminate due to the child attaining the limiting age for dependent children.
Proof of a child's incapacity and dependency must be furnished to the insurance company by the policyholder at least 30 days before
the child will attain the limiting age. The insurance company may require subsequent proof of the child's disability and dependency
during the 2 years following the child’s attainment of the limiting age. After that, the insurance company may require subsequent proof
not more than once each year.
