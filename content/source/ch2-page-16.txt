1. Solicitation and Sales Presentations
The process of issuing a life insurance policy begins with solicitation. In simplest terms, solicitation of insurance means an attempt to
persuade a person to buy an insurance policy, and it can be done orally or in writing. This includes providing information about
available products, describing the policy benefits, making recommendations about a specific type of policy, and trying to secure a
contract between the applicant and the insurance company.
Any sales presentations used by insurers or their agents in communication with the public must be accurate and complete.
To ensure that customers are given all the information they need to choose the best life insurance for their needs, the state places
specific requirements on the information insurers must give their customers.
These rules apply to any solicitation, negotiation or procurement of life insurance within the state and apply to any issuer of life
insurance contracts including fraternal benefit societies.
Exempt from solicitation rules are
* Annuities;
* Credit life insurance;
* Group life insurance;
* Life insurance policies issued in connection with pension and welfare plans subject to the federal Employee Retirement Income
Security Act of 1974 (ERISA); or
* Variable life insurance under which the amount and duration of the death benefits and cash values vary according to the
investment experience of a separate account.
