Advertising
First and foremost, advertising must be accurate and not misrepresent the facts. Advertising rules apply to any insurance
advertisement intended for presentation, distribution, dissemination or other advertising use when used or made either directly or
indirectly by or on behalf of the insurance company.
Every insurance company must establish and maintain a system of control over the content, form, and method of dissemination of all
advertising of its policies. The insurer whose policies are advertised is responsible for all its advertisements, regardless of who wrote,
created, presented, or distributed them.
‘An advertisement may not contain descriptions of policy limitations, exceptions or reductions that are worded in a positive manner (for
example, describing a waiting period as a benefit-builder). Limitation or exceptions must accurately describe the negative features.
The advertisement for a policy that covers only one disease or a list of specified diseases cannot imply coverage beyond the terms of
the policy. This advertisement must prominently state the limited nature of the policy. The statement must be worded similar to the
following: “THIS IS A LIMITED POLICY," THIS IS A CANCER-ONLY POLICY,” or "THIS IS AN AUTOMOBILE ACCIDENT-ONLY POLICY.”
In addition, the use of the following terms is specifically prohibited in advertisements (unless they are true) as they may be misleading
to a consumer:

* Only, just, merely, minimum or similar to describe the applicability of any exceptions, reductions or exclusions;

* Extra pay, extra cash, extra income or similar to describe a benefit for which payment is conditional upon confinement in a hospital

or similar facility; and
* All, full, complete, unlimited or similar, in a manner which exaggerates the extent of any policy benefit.
